## Purpose

At the pin, every decision the engine raises reaches the user as one line of text. C5's
Activity lists a row for each; a plain permission ask carries *Allow once* and *Deny*, and
every other kind — a question, a plan, an elicitation, either dialog — carries *Go to
channel*, where nothing is waiting, because the channel column draws a category name, a
timestamp and a one-line summary and has no cards at all.

C6.3 makes the decision the thing the user acts on. Six card kinds and two dialog cards,
rendered from what C3 reduced and answered through the one mapping §8.4 states: a permission
card that shows the diff a `Write` would make and the scope an *Always allow* would take; a
question card with its options side by side; a plan card; an elicitation form; a task card
that can send its work to the background; a refusal-fallback dialog that retracts the messages
it concerns when it is settled and not before; an overage dialog that renders the engine's own
fallback frame as its outcome. Beside them, the Thread tab: one thread at a time, Slack-style,
over §7.5's five kinds, where replying to a card is a way of answering it and *Ask on the
side* asks without touching the transcript. And the two things that stop a channel before it
starts — the project-server consent sheet of §6.12 and the trust banner of §6.11 — become
surfaces with actions rather than states with words.

One component serves both hosts. Activity stops constructing a card of its own and renders
this one, so the mapping from a click to an `InboundAnswer` exists once in the tree.

## Grounding baseline

What is on `main` at the pin, read rather than taken on report. Each line is a fact this
child's design stands on; the file and symbol are named so a reviewer can check the claim.

- **The decision item carries the whole request.** `WireReducer.open(_:state:at:)`
  (`FleetKit/Sources/FleetTimeline/Reduce/WireReducer.swift:378–390`) builds
  `DecisionItem(… payload: request.raw)` — the *entire* `control_request` object as it
  arrived, not a projection of it. `kind`, `title`, `toolUseID` and `agentID` are derived
  beside it. This is the single most consequential fact for Y2: a card built from a
  `DecisionItem` alone can decode every field §8.4 needs, because C2's `CanUseToolFields`,
  `UserDialogFields` and `ElicitationFields` are public `Codable` types
  (`ClaudeWire/Sources/WireFrames/InboundRequests.swift:110–175`) and `payload` is exactly
  what they decode from.
- **C2 already models every field §8.4 names.** `CanUseToolFields` carries `tool_name`,
  `input`, `permission_suggestions`, `blocked_path`, `decision_reason`,
  `decision_reason_type`, `classifier_approvable`, `suppress_always_allow_rule`,
  `default_to_no`, `matched_ask_rule`, `title`, `display_name`, `tool_use_id`, `agent_id`,
  `description`, `requires_user_interaction`. `default_to_no` is a **permission** field, not
  a dialog one. `UserDialogFields` carries `dialog_kind`, `payload`, `tool_use_id`.
  `ElicitationFields` carries `requested_schema`, `message`, `mcp_server_name`, `title`,
  `display_name`, `description`, `mode`, `url`, `elicitation_id`.
- **The answer type is closed and complete.** `InboundAnswer`
  (`ClaudeWire/Sources/WireFrames/InboundAnswers.swift`) has `.permission(PermissionResult)`,
  `.dialog(DialogAnswer)`, `.elicitation(ElicitationAnswer)`, `.hookContinue`, `.mcpResponse`,
  `.error`, and encodes each to the exact body §8.4's *Answer* column names.
  `PermissionUpdate` has the six modelled variants plus `.unknown(JSONValue)`, which
  re-encodes verbatim — so a suggestion this build does not model still round-trips through
  an *Always allow*.
- **One action, one path.** `LifecycleAction.answer(RequestID, InboundAnswer)`
  (`FleetKit/Sources/FleetSessions/Types/Actions.swift:72`) is the only route;
  `ChannelSupervisor.answer` (`Lifecycle/ChannelSupervisor.swift:463`) consumes the id before
  the write and raises `LifecycleError.answerFailed` after a failed one.
  `LifecycleError.decisionGone` covers an id that is no longer live.
- **`DecisionItem.State` is the card's state, and C3 settles four of its five cases.** The
  reducer opens `.pending` from `.request`; `.policyAnswered(error:)` and `.requestCancelled`
  settle it (C3 spec, "*From `.request`*"); `.exited` marks the whole overlay `stale` and
  keeps it for display; `processReplaced` resets it. The fifth,
  `.answered(outcome:)`, is settled only by `HostSignal.decisionAnswered`
  (`Reduce/Overlay.swift:9`, applied at `Reduce/WireReducer.swift:80`).
- **Nothing emits `HostSignal`.** A tree-wide search finds `HostSignal` constructed in no
  source file outside its own declaration, and `StreamIngestion`
  (`FleetKit/Sources/FleetTimeline/Ingest/StreamIngestion.swift`) exposes `open`,
  `fileChanged`, `loadEarlier`, `relocated`, `close` and `rawRecord` — and no way to hand it
  one. See the `[parent-impact]` below; it is this child's largest finding.
- **The pane seam exists and refuses cleanly.** `PanelHost.run(_ request: PaneRequest) async
  throws` is on the protocol (`Workbench/Sources/PanelHostAPI/PanelHost.swift:51`) and
  `PanelHostModel.run` (`App/Panels/PanelHostModel.swift:358–366`) throws
  `PanelHostError.noPaneRunner(.terminal)` when no runner is registered. The composite's
  "surfaces the host's *no runner* refusal as a banner naming the terminal" is therefore
  implementable at the pin with no new seam.
- **An unrouted link falls back rather than vanishing.** `HostLinkRouter.open`
  (`App/Panels/HostLinkRouter.swift:59–74`) picks the most specific registered target and,
  finding none, takes `fallback(link)`: a `.url` goes to `openExternally`, everything else to
  a diagnostic naming the kind only. C7.3's Browser tab is not on `main`, so the overage
  card's billing route lands in the fallback — which is a degraded item 62, not a dropped one.
- **Activity's inline answer is one function already.** `ActivityModel.answer(_:to:on:)`
  (`App/Activity/ActivityModel.swift`) performs `.answer` and nothing else; `allowOnce` and
  `deny` are its two callers, and `ActivityItem.ask` is non-nil for exactly one shape — a live
  `can_use_tool` without `requires_user_interaction`, read from
  `ChannelEventPump.requests[id]`, because `PendingDecision`
  (`FleetKit/Sources/FleetSessions/Types/ChannelState.swift:82`) carries no payload.
- **The Y1 registry is total but its builder is lossy.** `RowRegistry.RowBuilder` is
  `@MainActor (TimelineRow) -> AnyView` and `TimelineRow`
  (`App/Timeline/ChannelTimelineModel.swift:46–57`) is `id`, `category`, `timestamp` and a
  one-line `summary`. A decision row cannot be drawn from it. See the residue below.
- **`side_question` is modelled with its history.** `SideQuestion`
  (`ClaudeWire/Sources/WireFrames/OutboundRequests.swift:126`) takes `question` and a
  `history` array whose elements are `{question, response, fallback_notice?}` and omits the
  key entirely when empty; `LifecycleAPI.send(AnyControlRequest, on:)` is its route. X10's
  `/btw` strategy builds one *without* history, so the Thread tab's accumulating variant is
  additive, not a duplicate.
- **`make check-wiring`** (`Tools/c5/check-app-wiring.py`, tracker 72) makes a member declared
  under `App/` whose only callers are under `AppTests/` a finding. Nothing this child ships
  may be reachable only from a test.
- **Fixtures** this child replays: `ask-user-question`, `dialog-refusal-fallback`,
  `dialog-fable-overage`, `permission-allow`, `permission-deny`, `exit-plan-mode`,
  `send-user-file`, `background-shell`, `notification-hook`, `control-shapes`, and
  `fake-claude` for the arms no recording carries.
- **`main` has moved since the pin** — `ece4fa8`, the tracker-77 roster corrective and the
  dispatch bookkeeping. None of it touches this child's fence; it is merged in at Task 1 and
  the parent-pin stays `5e24f1a`.

## Residue

Everything below is what the composite's C6.3 section and its inheritance leave open. Each
carries a recommendation. The ones marked **[architect]** are escalated with the spec and
block nothing until execution reaches them; the rest this child decides and records.

### D1 — [architect, blocking at the last task] The Y1 row builder cannot carry a decision

`RowBuilder` takes a `TimelineRow`, which is four scalars. A decision card needs
`DecisionItem.payload`, `.state`, `.kind`, `.toolUseID` and `.agentID`; the sent-file item
needs `SentFileItem.files`, `.caption` and `.delivered`. Neither survives the projection.

Three ways out. **(a)** the builder's parameter becomes `TimelineItem`, and
`PlaceholderRowView` derives its `TimelineRow` from the item — a five-line change confined to
`App/Timeline/Rows/RowRegistry.swift`, which is skeleton and belongs to the composite. **(b)**
`TimelineRow` gains `let item: TimelineItem` — same effect, but it widens a value type C6.1's
list is virtualized over and that C6.1 may want to keep narrow for S7. **(c)** the builder
looks the item up by `ItemID` through a model handle — a second lookup path and a lifetime
question for no gain.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is the smallest change, it is confined to a file the composite
owns, and it removes a projection that exists for no reason — every leaf's builder wants the
item and one of them cannot use a summary string. Who lands it is the architect's: C6.1 owns
eleven of thirteen kinds and the column, so C6.1 landing it as part of its first task is the
cheapest ordering, and this child's in-timeline row (its last task) then depends on C6.1's
merge exactly as the composite's Ordering already says it does. **This child builds and tests
its card against `DecisionItem` directly and is unaffected until that task.**

### D2 — [architect, blocking at the last task] `HostSignal` has no delivery path

Y2 is explicit: "A card's *state* … is C3's `DecisionItem.state`; the view renders it and
never keeps its own." That cannot hold at the pin. The only transition into
`.answered(outcome:)` is `HostSignal.decisionAnswered`, `HostSignal` is constructed nowhere in
the tree, and `StreamIngestion` — the app-side owner of the wire reducer — exposes no member
that accepts one. A user who answers a card in the timeline today would see it stay `pending`
for ever.

This is not this child's to fix and it is not only this child's problem: C6.2's composer needs
`promptSent` for `TurnSummaryItem`'s `.prompted` attribution and `rewound` for item 13.
**Recommendation: a C3 corrective on `main`, in the same shape as the tracker-74 and -77
correctives the composite's Risks section already schedules —** `StreamIngestion` gains
`public func signal(_ HostSignal) async -> Effect`, routed to `WireReducer.apply(_:at:)`, which
is already public and already correct. The app-side caller is one line after a successful
`perform(.answer)`. Filed below as a `[parent-impact]` against X4 and X5 because the two
contracts disagree about who closes the loop, and X5's `perform(.answer)` returning a
`ChannelState` is the shape that made the gap easy to miss.

**Fallback if the corrective does not land before this child's last task:** the card renders
`.pending` with the answer it sent shown as *sent*, taken from a per-channel answered set the
component owns, and the divergence from Y2 is recorded as a tracker entry with the corrective
named as its closer. This is stated so the leaf never blocks; it is not the preferred answer,
because a view keeping decision state is exactly what Y2 exists to forbid.

### D3 — [architect] Where this child's channel-level surfaces mount

The trust banner, the consent sheet and the in-timeline card all have to appear inside the
channel column, and `App/Views/ChannelColumnView.swift` is C5's file that the composite says
"C6 replaces whole" without assigning it to a leaf. `make check-wiring` forbids shipping the
views unmounted.

**Recommendation:** this child mounts a single entry point — `ChannelDecorations(channel:)`,
declared in `App/Consent/`, drawing the trust banner, the consent sheet presenter and this
child's answer-failure line — with one insertion into whichever view owns the channel column
at the time it merges, and the fence is widened by that one insertion. Because the merge order
is C6.1 then C6.3, the file this child edits is the one C6.1 already replaced, so there is no
conflict to resolve and nothing for C6.1 to preserve. Asked rather than assumed because it is
a fence change, and a fence a leaf widens for itself is not a fence.

### D4 — [architect] Y2 versus C5's human-gate ruling 4

Y2 says the timeline and Activity "both host that one view". C5's human-gate ruling 4
(2026-09-06, C5 spec, *Human-gate rulings*) says Activity shows **rows for every decision
kind** and offers an inline answer **only for permission prompts**; every other kind opens the
channel, "where C6's card lives". Read literally, Y2 reverses a ruling the human gave.

They reconcile, and the reconciliation is almost certainly what Y2 meant: what Activity adopts
is the **component and its answer mapping**, not the full-height card and not a widened set of
kinds. `ActivityRowView` renders `DecisionCardView` in a compact presentation for a plain
permission ask — so *Allow once* and *Deny* come from the one mapping rather than from
`ActivityModel`'s own two functions — and every other kind keeps its row and its *Go to
channel*. Y2's stated invariant ("the answer path is one function"; G3's static test that
`ActivityModel` constructs no card of its own) is satisfied exactly; the human's ruling is
untouched.

**Recommendation: adopt the reconciliation, and record it as a Parent revision on the
composite** so Y2's wording says *the component and its mapping*, not *the view*. If the
architect reads Y2 as genuinely widening Activity, that is a product decision that goes back
to the human, because it reverses an answered gate question.

### D5 — *Always allow* offers three destinations, not five

§8.4: "*Always allow* … with `updatedPermissions` at the chosen `destination` (user, project
or local settings)". `PermissionUpdateDestination` has five cases; `session` and `cliArg` are
not places a user chooses to persist a rule. **Decided:** the picker offers exactly
`userSettings`, `projectSettings` and `localSettings`; the destination carried by the
engine's own suggestion is preselected when it is one of those three, and `localSettings`
otherwise; the suggestion re-encodes with only its `destination` replaced, so a suggestion of
a variant this build does not model (`.unknown`) is **not** offered under *Always allow* at
all — its shape is unknown, so its destination field is unknown, and rewriting a field of an
opaque value is guessing. A card whose suggestions are all `.unknown` shows *Allow once* and
*Deny…* only, and says why in one line.

### D6 — A denial does not interrupt

`PermissionResult.deny(message:interrupt:classification:)` carries an `interrupt` flag §8.4
never mentions. **Decided:** `interrupt: false` everywhere in this child. A denial is
feedback the model reads and works around — item 41 requires exactly that ("Claude's next
message reflects the reason") — and stopping the turn is a separate affordance the user
already has (Esc, §8.7). Activity's landed path already spells it this way.

### D7 — The billing route degrades through the link fallback

Item 62 wants *Set up usage credits…* to open the Anthropic billing page "in the Browser tab".
C7.3 is not on `main`, and the composite names conditional edges to C7.2 and C7.4 but not to
C7.3. **Decided:** the action emits `WorkspaceLink.url` through `ChannelContext.links` and
asserts nothing about the destination; with no Browser target registered, `HostLinkRouter`'s
fallback opens it externally, which is the parent's item 62 degraded rather than skipped —
the same bargain the composite struck for item 47. Recorded as a third conditional edge, on
C7.3, in the Parent revisions below. **The URL is not invented here**: it comes from the
dialog payload if the engine supplies one, and if it does not, the card offers no billing
action and says the terminal is where credits are set up. (Which of the two holds is settled
by the bundle reading in the Design; a hard-coded console URL would be afleet asserting a
first-party fact it has no evidence for.)

### D8 — The elicitation form implements a stated subset

`requested_schema` is JSON Schema and the engine does not bound it. **Decided:** the form
renders `type: object` with `properties` of `string` (text field; `enum` becomes a picker;
`format: uri`/`email` unvalidated), `number`/`integer` (numeric field), and `boolean`
(toggle), honouring `required`, `title`, `description` and `default`. Anything else — a
nested object, an array, a `oneOf` — renders as a raw JSON text field for that property, with
the property's own schema shown, and the card says the form is partial. `decline` and
`cancel` are always available whatever the schema is, because §6.4 makes an unanswerable
request the one failure mode that must not exist. The subset and its boundary are a tracker
entry so the next widening has a place to start.

### D9 — The diff is `CollectionDifference` over lines, behind `DiffRendering`

**Decided:** `protocol DiffRendering { func view(before: String, after: String, path: String)
-> AnyView }`, with `AttributedDiffRenderer` computing a line-level
`CollectionDifference` and drawing added, removed and context lines. `Write` diffs against the
file's current contents when it exists and against empty when it does not — read with
`String(contentsOf:)` and no descriptor held open on a user-content directory (the C5 TCC
finding). `Edit` diffs `old_string` against `new_string` in place, with the surrounding lines
from the file for context. When C7.2's `MonacoEditorView` is on `main`, a second conformer
replaces the renderer and no card changes. A file the app cannot read renders the tool input
verbatim rather than a diff, and says so.

### D10 — Reply-to-card and *Ask on the side* send through X5 directly

§7.5's Decision thread replies *are* answers: a reply to a permission card is
`perform(.answer(id, .permission(.deny(message: <the text>, …))))`, to a plan card the
rejection with the text as feedback, to a question card *Other* with the text. Those need no
composer. The tool-detail and sent-file threads post to the main session with a `Re: <tool>
<short id>:` prefix, which is `perform(.send(UserInput(text:)))` — a plain text send.
**Decided:** the Thread tab calls `perform(.send)` for those two kinds with a one-line field
and no router, no `@`, no `!`, no attachments; every one of those belongs to C6.2's composer
and this child ships none of them. Recorded as a seam so C6.2's composer is not duplicated and
so a later child that wants the full composer in a thread knows where to put it.
*Ask on the side* is `lifecycle.send(AnyControlRequest(SideQuestion(question:history:)))`, the
history accumulated in the thread's own state as `{question, response, fallback_notice?}`
objects in ask order, and the thread asserts item 10's negative directly: no record reaches
the main transcript.

### D11 — Retraction is a filter this child owns and C6.1 applies

§8.4's `retractedMessageUuids` name already-streamed messages that vanish **on resolution**.
Removing them is not reduction — §7.3's differential invariant forbids this unit adding a
reducer, and the composite's Parent-Level Acceptance restates it — so it is a render-time
filter. **Decided:** `App/Decisions/RetractionRegistry.swift`, `@MainActor`, keyed by channel,
holding the uuid set of every *resolved* refusal dialog, with a pure
`func retains(_ item: TimelineItem) -> Bool` the channel's list calls. This child drives and
asserts the registry directly and mounts the filter at its last task, beside D1's row. **C6.1
must call it**; noted for the architect as the one thing this child needs from C6.1 beyond
the row slot. The registry is populated on the answer, not on the receipt, and a
`control_cancel_request` that retires the dialog counts as a resolution (§8.4).

### D12 — Two inert states, two words

Item 42 wants "answered elsewhere"; item 44 wants "session ended". `DecisionItem.State` gives
`.cancelled` (from `.requestCancelled`, which is the binary's `control_cancel_request` — item
42) and, for item 44, the process is gone: `.exited` marks the whole overlay `stale` and the
item keeps whatever state it had. **Decided:** the card's inert wording reads from the pair
`(state, overlay.stale)` — `.cancelled` renders *answered elsewhere*, and a `.pending` card in
a stale overlay renders *session ended*. `.policyAnswered(error:)` renders the error as its
outcome (item 45's row is C6.1's opaque row; the card is only reached if the reducer opened
one, which for an unknown subtype it does not). Both are asserted in G1; the second is the
discriminating one, because a card that read `state` alone would render *waiting* on a dead
process.

### D13 — Six live turns, and what happens when one fails

The composite caps this leaf at six turns for items 4, 5, 6, 7 and 41, "each live item at most
twice". Those two clauses are in tension: the five items need six turns as written (item 5
alone needs two — the *Always allow* file and the third file that must draw no card), so a
single re-run exceeds the cap. **Decided, and reported rather than assumed:** the pass is
scripted as exactly six turns in one isolated channel; a turn that fails is re-run **once**,
for that item only, and the seventh turn is reported as an overage against the composite's
ten-dollar ceiling with the reason. A second failure is a finding, never a third run. If the
architect would rather the cap be hard, the fallback is to record the failure without re-run;
say so and this child does that instead.

The script, in order, in a disposable directory under `/private/tmp/afleet-fixtures/` with
*Isolated settings* on (`--setting-sources ""`, plus `--strict-mcp-config` if the directory
declares servers) and `default` mode:

| Turn | Prompt | Item | What the card must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Create a file named ask.txt containing hello` | 4 | `Write` card with path and content; *Allow once* → `allow`, `user_temporary`; the file appears |
| 2 | `Create a file named ask2.txt containing hello` | 5 | *Always allow* offered because the request carries suggestions; → `allow` with `updatedPermissions`, `user_permanent` |
| 3 | `Create a file named ask3.txt containing hello` | 5 | no card; the file appears |
| 4 | `Create a file named nope.txt containing hello` | 41 | *Deny…* with `not now`; no file; the card shows denied; the next message reflects the reason |
| 5 | `Use AskUserQuestion to ask me whether I prefer tabs or spaces` | 6 | question card; selecting an option continues the turn with that answer |
| 6 | (`plan` mode) `Plan a hello-world script` | 7 | plan card; *Approve* sends `allow` with `updatedPermissions: [setMode]` and the picker follows |

Turn 6 needs the mode picker, which is C6.2's. **Decided:** this child sets the mode for turn
6 through `lifecycle.send(AnyControlRequest(SetPermissionMode(...)))` in the gate's own
harness, not through a picker it ships — the gate is about the plan *card*, and building a
picker to reach it would be C6.2's work done twice.

Everything else is a fixture. Items 42, 43, 44, 45, 57, 61 and 62 replay through
`fake-claude`; item 37's reply-to-card and item 10's side question are fixture arms; the
consent sheet and the trust banner run on a lifecycle double. Nothing in this child sends
`submit_feedback`.

### D14 — Activity constructs its `DecisionItem`, and does not construct a card

Activity is fleet-wide and holds no `ChannelTimeline` for a channel the user has not opened,
so it cannot read the overlay. It holds the live `InboundRequest` in its pump. **Decided:** an
app-side `DecisionItem(surfacing: InboundRequest, in: ChannelKey)` initialiser in
`App/Decisions/` builds the item Activity renders — same `payload: request.raw`, same derived
`kind`, `state: .pending` — so both hosts hand the component the same type and G3's static
test ("`ActivityModel` constructs no card of its own") has a shape to assert. The derivation
duplicates four small `WireReducer` statics; the alternative is a FleetKit addition outside
this child's fence for four lines, and the duplication is covered by a test that the two
agree on every fixture request.

## Acceptance

The composite's five gates, restated as what a test or a person can observe. Three rules bind
every one.

**Discriminating tests (root §17.7, binding).** A test written to prove a fix is demonstrated
failing against the pre-fix code before the fix is accepted. Where the break cannot be
executed, the substitute is a trace assertion that the dangerous path was never entered,
stated as such. Reviewers ask what an assertion would have to *see* to fail — an assertion
that a card disappeared is not an assertion that the right answer was sent.

**Nothing is written under a config home (X9).** Not by this child, not by its tests. The
default suite builds its scratch trees under `FileManager.default.temporaryDirectory`. The one
live gate (G5) runs under `/tmp/afleet-fixtures/config-home` with C5's `ScratchLiveGate` and
the recursive `ConfigHomeWitness`, and the working directory is a disposable directory under
`/private/tmp/afleet-fixtures/` that the scratch home already trusts. The one documented
write exception — a declined `.mcp.json` server into the project's
`.claude/settings.local.json` (§6.12) — goes through `LifecycleAPI.declineProjectServers` and
never through code this child ships.

**Reports emit counts (root §6.3, §11).** Every gate report, failure message and diagnostic
states counts, sizes and durations — never a path, a title, a session id or a slug. Test
inputs use invented identifiers.

### G1 (required) — every card kind, every dialog, every answer asserted as the answer

Driven through `fake-claude` and a lifecycle double; the assertion in every clause is the
exact `InboundAnswer` value handed to `perform(.answer)`, never the card's disappearance.

- **G1a permission, items 4 and 5.** A `Write` ask with `permission_suggestions`:
  *Allow once* → `.permission(.allow(updatedInput: nil, updatedPermissions: nil,
  classification: .userTemporary))`; *Always allow* at each of the three destinations →
  `.allow` carrying the suggestion re-encoded at that destination, classification
  `.userPermanent`; *Deny…* with text → `.deny(message: <text>, interrupt: false,
  classification: .userReject)`. With `suppress_always_allow_rule` set, and with no
  suggestions, *Always allow* is absent — both halves asserted, because a card that never
  offered it would pass the first alone. With `default_to_no`, decline is focused and no
  approve shortcut is bound. With `requires_user_interaction`, no one-tap approve or deny
  exists at all. `decision_reason` is rendered stripped of ANSI, and rebuilt from
  `decision_reason_type` and `matched_ask_rule` when empty (*A-24*).
- **G1b question, item 57.** The `ask-user-question` fixture: options render with their
  previews side by side, multi-select where the input allows it, *Other* takes text; the
  answer is `.allow` with `updatedInput` equal to the request's whole input plus the

## Design

### One value, one view, one function

```
DecisionItem  ──decode──►  DecisionCard  ──►  DecisionCardView(.full | .compact)
 (C3, overlay)              (a value)              │
                                                   ▼  DecisionAction (a closed set)
                                                   │
                                            answer(_:) -> InboundAnswer?
                                                   │
                                          DecisionAnswering
                                                   │
                                   perform(.answer(id, answer), on: key)
```

`DecisionCard` is decoded from `DecisionItem` and holds no reference to a channel, a
lifecycle or a view. Its payload case is typed with C2's own `Codable` fields — the whole
point of `DecisionItem.payload` being `request.raw` is that no second source is needed. A
payload this build cannot type becomes `.unmodelled(JSONValue)` and renders as a read-only
card with no answer actions, because §6.3's rule for an unknown *inbound request* is an
immediate error answer at the transport, not a guess at the surface; a card only exists at
all for a subtype the reducer opened one for.

`DecisionAction` is closed. `answer(_:)` is a pure function from an action to an
`InboundAnswer?`, and it is the **only** place in this child that constructs one. That is
what makes Y2 checkable rather than aspirational: two hosts render one view, one view emits
one action set, one function turns an action into an answer, and one object performs it.
`answer(_:)` returns nil for the two actions that are not answers — the overage card's
billing link and the task card's *Move to background*, which is a control request — so
"every answer comes from here" is an assertion a test can make about the shape of the code
and not only about its behaviour.

`DecisionAnswering` is the object both hosts hold. It performs `.answer`, renders a
`LifecycleError` through C5's `RowBanner`, and tells the channel's pump to forget the id —
the seam C5 already installed, because the engine sends no frame back for an answer.

### The two presentations

`.full` is the timeline's card: title, consent line, the tool's input formatted per §8.4
(shell in monospace, `Edit` and `Write` as diffs, paths as links through
`ChannelContext.links`), the actions as buttons, the outcome when it is inert. `.compact` is
Activity's row affordance: the tool's display name and the same action buttons, without the
input rendering, because Activity is a list of everything waiting across the fleet and a
full card per row is a different surface. Both draw from the same `DecisionCard` and emit
the same `DecisionAction`. The presentation changes the layout and nothing else — no action
exists in one and not the other for the kinds Activity answers, which is the invariant G3's
byte-identical-answer test holds.

### The state is C3's, and where that is not yet true

The card renders `DecisionItem.state` and keeps none of its own. The five states map to five
readings: `.pending` (actions live), `.answered(outcome:)` (inert, the outcome shown),
`.cancelled` (inert, *answered elsewhere*), `.policyAnswered(error:)` (inert, the error),
`.inert`. The sixth reading is not a state: a `.pending` card in an overlay C3 has marked
`stale` reads *session ended*, which is item 44. Until D2's corrective lands, `.answered` is
unreachable and `DecisionAnswering` shows the answer as *sent* from its own in-flight set for
the duration of the round trip; the tracker entry names the corrective as its closer.

### The permission card

`display_name` or `tool_name` as the title; `description` as the consent line;
`ToolInput.parse` for the input, so `Bash` renders its command in monospace, `Read` and
`Write` render their path as a link, and `Edit`/`Write` render a diff (D9). `decision_reason`
is stripped of ANSI and, when empty, rebuilt from `decision_reason_type` and
`matched_ask_rule` (*A-24*). `agent_id` joins to the run's type and description as C3 already
joins them, which is item 52's card half and the same label the Agents tab will draw through
Y2.

Three actions, and which of them exist is data: *Allow once* always; *Always allow* only when
`permission_suggestions` is non-empty, `suppress_always_allow_rule` is unset, and at least one
suggestion is a modelled variant (D5); *Deny…* always, opening a text field. `default_to_no`
focuses decline and unbinds the approve shortcut. `requires_user_interaction` removes the
one-tap approve and deny entirely: the tool's own card — the question card, in the one case
the engine ships — is the surface, and this is why `AskUserQuestion` arrives as a
`can_use_tool` and is nonetheless a different card.

### The two dialog cards

`UserDialogFields.dialogKind` selects the card; the two kinds afleet declares in the
handshake are the two it renders, and any other kind is left unanswered by design (§6.3) and
produces no card. The refusal card's actions send their result inside `{behavior:
"completed", result: <string>}`; closing sends `{behavior: "cancelled"}`, which is also the
binary's own default at its dialog deadline. The overage card offers `consent` only when
`overagesEnabled` is true, because a bare wire reply never enables billing; with it false the
first action is a billing link that leaves the card **pending** — the only action in this
child that resolves nothing — and the card resolves on *Switch to the default model* or *Not
now*. A following `system/model_consent_fallback` renders its `content` as the card's outcome
whatever the answer was.

`retractedMessageUuids` are held by `RetractionRegistry` and applied at render time by the
channel's list, never by a reducer (D11). They enter the registry on *resolution* — any
answer, or a `control_cancel_request` that retires the dialog — and never on receipt, which
is the clause item 62 asserts in both directions.

### The Thread tab

A `PanelTab` for `.thread`, taken from C5's placeholder by `unregister` then `register`
(Y3). One thread open at a time, Slack-style, in a `PanelTabSession` the host retains per
(tab, channel) so a channel switch does not lose it. Five kinds, five anchors, five reply
behaviours as §7.5's table states them. The three answerable kinds reply through
`answer(_:)` — a reply *is* the card's textual outcome, so the same function produces it and
G2's assertion is on the emitted answer. The two posting kinds send a plain
`perform(.send(UserInput))` with the `Re: <tool> <short id>:` prefix and no composer
machinery (D10). *Ask on the side* is `SideQuestion` through
`LifecycleAPI.send(AnyControlRequest, on:)` with history accumulated in the thread's own
state.

### Consent and trust

One `ChannelDecorations` view over `LifecycleAPI.preconditions(for:)`. `consentNeeded`
presents the sheet: each server's name and transport summary, *Accept* →
`acceptProjectServers` (which writes nothing; the store remembers the acceptance per project
and server hash), *Decline* → `declineProjectServers` (the one Claude Code-owned file afleet
writes, and it is C4 that writes it). `LifecycleError.declineRefused` renders §6.12's
fail-closed banner pointing at the terminal's `/mcp` flow. `untrusted(root:)` renders the
banner — naming the project, never the path (§11) — with *Review trust in terminal*:
`openInTerminal` for the `PaneRequest`, handed to `PanelHost.run` unchanged with its `id`,
and `PanelHostError.noPaneRunner` surfaced as a banner naming the terminal. That is item 47
degraded exactly as far as C7.4's absence forces and no further.

### The sent-file item

`SentFileItem` renders its caption, its file list and a preview of the first file, with
*Open in Files* as a `WorkspaceLink.file(url, line: nil)` through the context's `links`
(item 29). `delivered` renders as the item's status. The preview reads the file's head with
no descriptor held on a user-content directory.

### Engine anchors

C2's models already carry the bundle citations for the request and answer schemas
(`InboundRequests.swift` and `InboundAnswers.swift` cite `cli.pretty.js:513878`, schemas `KL`,

1. the two declared dialog kinds' payload field names and the exact result strings the engine

2. the dialog default (`cancelled`) and the deadline at which the binary cancels an unanswered

## `[parent-impact]` — X4 and X5 leave `HostSignal` unreachable

**Where.** Root §17.5's X4 makes the timeline model C3's and names the overlay; X5 makes
`perform(.answer)` the one answer path and returns a `ChannelState`. Neither says who tells
the reducer that an answer was sent, and at `5e24f1a` nobody does: `HostSignal` is constructed
in no source file, and `StreamIngestion` has no member that takes one. Five host facts —
`promptSent`, `decisionAnswered`, `rewound`, `processReplaced`, `relocated` — are modelled,
reduced (`Reduce/WireReducer.swift:74–95`) and unreachable. Only `relocated` has an alternate
route, through `StreamIngestion.relocated(mainPath:)`.

**Why it is the parent's.** It spans two contracts and three children. C6.3's cards cannot
leave `pending`; C6.2's turn attribution and item 13 need `promptSent` and `rewound`; C3 owns
the type and C4 owns the action that should raise it. No leaf can close it inside its fence.

**Recommended fix.** A C3 corrective on `main`, the shape the composite's Risks section
already uses for tracker 74 and 77: `StreamIngestion` gains
`public func signal(_ HostSignal) async -> Effect`, forwarding to `WireReducer.apply(_:at:)`
and publishing the resulting changes through the same effects stream every other mutation
uses; the app raises it after a successful `perform(.answer)`. X4 gains one sentence naming
the delivery path; X5 gains one naming the app as the raiser.

## `[parent-impact]` — the composite's conditional edges name C7.2 and C7.4, not C7.3

**Where.** The composite's *Risks* and *Ordering* name two conditional edges. Item 62's
billing route is a third: it wants the Browser tab, which is C7.3's. **Recommended fix:** add
C7.3 to the conditional-edge list with the fallback D7 states — the link is emitted through
X7's router and the host's own unrouted-`.url` fallback opens it externally until the Browser
tab registers a target. Advisory in effect, filed for completeness because a reader of the
composite would otherwise think item 62 is unconditional.
